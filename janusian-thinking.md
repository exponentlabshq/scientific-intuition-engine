Yes. Rothenberg’s definition is narrower than “thinking dialectically”: **Janusian thinking means actively conceiving two or more contradictory ideas, concepts, or images simultaneously**. His scientific-creativity research explicitly identifies examples involving Einstein, Bohr, Darwin, Dirac, Planck, Yukawa, and Nobel laureates including McMillan, Bloembergen, Corey, de Gennes, Gilbert, Herschbach, Kornberg, Lehn, Lederberg, Purcell, and Townes. ([OUP Academic][1])

The most famous documented cases are **Einstein’s falling observer—motion/rest—and Bohr’s wave/particle complementarity**. ([PubMed][2])

### The important distinction

Rothenberg's strongest examples are not merely **“A resembles B.”** They are closer to:

> **A is true, and its apparent opposite is simultaneously true. Now build the theory that can contain both.**

That makes the three mechanisms quite cleanly separable:

| Cognitive mechanism                   | Core operation                                                      | Signature sentence                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Bisociation — Koestler**            | Two previously separate matrices collide                            | **“What works here might work there.”**                                              |
| **Janusian thinking — Rothenberg**    | Two opposites are conceived simultaneously                          | **“Both of these apparently incompatible things are true.”**                         |
| **Homospatial thinking — Rothenberg** | Distinct entities occupy the same conceptual/spatial representation | **“Put these separate things in the same place and see what new identity emerges.”** |

And Rothenberg's empirical program is unusually relevant to what you're doing: he did not merely propose Janusian thinking philosophically; he interviewed **22 Nobel laureates**, examined historical records of scientists including **Einstein, Bohr, Darwin, Dirac, Planck, and Yukawa**, and reported Janusian formulations in their creative work. ([Taylor & Francis Online][5])

The **Einstein case is particularly powerful** for your framework because Rothenberg had documentary evidence of the actual sequence of Einstein's thinking leading to general relativity, rather than merely interpreting the finished theory after the fact. ([PubMed][2])
