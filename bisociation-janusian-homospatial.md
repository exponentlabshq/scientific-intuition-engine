Yes. There is a useful distinction to preserve here:

**Rothenberg’s Janusian process is the strongest of the three as an empirically documented Nobel-laureate phenomenon.** His work studied 22 Nobel laureates and documentary records of scientists including Einstein, Bohr, Darwin, Dirac, Planck, and Yukawa. He defines Janusian thinking as **actively conceiving multiple opposites simultaneously**; homospatial thinking as **conceiving distinct entities in the same mental space**. ([OUP Academic][1])

For the **modern** tables below, I use those definitions as analytical lenses. **“Documented” means Rothenberg specifically identified the mechanism; “reconstruction” means the Nobel-winning discovery strongly exhibits the mechanism, but I am not claiming the laureate literally described their thought in those terms.**

## 1. Modern Janusian thinking

**The Janusian move:** *hold X and not-X simultaneously, then construct a theory in which both can be true.*

|  2 | **Bohr — quantum complementarity**               | Wave                     | Particle                 | One quantum object requires incompatible descriptions                                | **“Light cannot be reduced to wave or particle; the contradiction is part of the phenomenon.”**                                                 |
|  3 | **Heisenberg — uncertainty**                     | Precise position         | Precise momentum         | Increasing one precision excludes the other                                          | **“The more precisely I define one quantity, the less precisely the other can exist in the same description.”**                                 |
|  4 | **Dirac — antimatter**                           | Positive-energy solution | Negative-energy solution | Mathematical contradiction becomes physical prediction                               | **“The equation gives me something impossible; rather than discard it, I will ask what particle could make the impossible solution physical.”** |
|  5 | **Planck — quantum hypothesis**                  | Continuity               | Discreteness             | Continuous radiation exchanges discrete quanta                                       | **“The field behaves continuously while energy arrives in packets; nature can be continuous and discrete at once.”**                            |

|  9 | **Allison & Honjo — cancer immunotherapy**       | Immune activation        | Immune inhibition        | Cancer therapy works by removing a brake rather than pushing harder                  | **“To attack the tumor, I need not strengthen the immune system; I can stop inhibiting it.”** ([Nobel Prize][4])                                |

The **Yamanaka, Karikó–Weissman, Allison–Honjo, graphene, and topological examples are especially good modern cases** because the productive tension is not merely rhetorical: the apparent contradiction is built into the scientific result itself. ([Nobel Prize][2])

# 2. Modern bisociation

**The bisociative move:** *take a structure or rule operating in one matrix of thought and make it operate in another.*

This is Koestler's territory: the same event is made to “vibrate” in two ordinarily incompatible frames of reference. ([Springer Nature Link][9])

|  # | Nobel work / laureate                          | Domain A              | Domain B              | Bisociative bridge                               | Prose reconstruction                                                                                                                                          |
| -: | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1 | **Thouless / Kosterlitz — topology in matter** | Pure mathematics      | Condensed matter      | Topological invariants → physical phases         | **“Topology is abstract geometry until I let its invariants describe real materials.”**                                                                       |
|  2 | **Laughlin — fractional quantum Hall effect**  | Quantum mechanics     | Fluid mechanics       | Collective electron behavior → quantum fluid     | **“Electrons confined to a plane behave less like individual particles and more like a new kind of fluid.”** ([Nobel Prize][5])                               |
|  3 | **Hopfield — neural networks**                 | Statistical physics   | Neuroscience          | Energy minimization → memory retrieval           | **“A brain can be understood like a physical system settling into an energy minimum.”**                                                                       |
|  4 | **Metropolis — simulated annealing**           | Thermodynamics        | Optimization          | Cooling → computational search                   | **“Let an algorithm cool like matter: randomness first, convergence later.”**                                                                                 |
|  5 | **Holland — genetic algorithms**               | Evolution             | Computation           | Selection/recombination → optimization           | **“Evolution is already searching a solution space; I can make the computer search the same way.”**                                                           |
|  6 | **Sutton & Barto — reinforcement learning**    | Animal learning       | Machine learning      | Reward/punishment → algorithm                    | **“Instead of programming the answer, let the machine learn from consequences.”**                                                                             |
|  7 | **Shannon — information theory**               | Communication         | Statistical mechanics | Uncertainty → entropy/information                | **“The uncertainty of a message can be measured with the same mathematics used to describe entropy.”**                                                        |
|  8 | **Feynman — quantum computation**              | Quantum physics       | Computer science      | Quantum state evolution → computation            | **“Nature appears to compute quantum mechanically; perhaps computation should exploit the same substrate.”**                                                  |
|  9 | **Murray Gell-Mann / quark model**             | Particle physics      | Symmetry/group theory | Mathematical symmetry → particle classification  | **“Instead of treating the particles as a zoo, let symmetry tell me how the zoo is organized.”**                                                              |
| 10 | **Prigogine — nonequilibrium thermodynamics**  | Thermodynamics        | Chemistry/biology     | Dissipation → organization                       | **“A thermodynamic system can use energy flow to create structure rather than merely lose it.”**                                                              |
| 11 | **Lorenz — chaos**                             | Meteorology           | Nonlinear mathematics | Weather → dynamical systems                      | **“The atmosphere is not merely unpredictable weather; it is a nonlinear dynamical system.”**                                                                 |
| 12 | **Mandelbrot — fractal economics**             | Geometry              | Financial markets     | Self-similarity → price dynamics                 | **“The irregular shape of a price series may obey the same scaling logic as irregular natural forms.”**                                                       |
| 13 | **Goodenough — battery materials**             | Solid-state physics   | Energy storage        | Crystal structure → electrochemistry             | **“The geometry of a crystal can become the mechanism by which a battery stores and releases energy.”** ([Nobel Prize][8])                                    |
| 14 | **Karikó & Weissman — mRNA**                   | RNA biochemistry      | Immunology            | Molecular modification → immune evasion          | **“A chemical modification known from RNA biology becomes an immunological switch.”** ([Nobel Prize][3])                                                      |
| 15 | **zur Hausen — HPV/cancer**                    | Virology              | Oncology              | Viral genetics → carcinogenesis                  | **“A cancer cell can be read as a historical record of a virus living inside its genome.”** ([Nobel Prize][10])                                               |
| 16 | **Yamanaka — reprogramming**                   | Developmental biology | Genetic engineering   | Transcription factors → reversal of cell fate    | **“The factors that maintain a cell's identity can also be used to erase that identity.”** ([Nobel Prize][2])                                                 |
| 17 | **Geim & Novoselov — graphene**                | Everyday materials    | Quantum physics       | Pencil graphite → two-dimensional quantum system | **“A material sitting inside an ordinary pencil can become a laboratory for new quantum physics once isolated as a single atomic layer.”** ([Nobel Prize][7]) |

The difference from Janusian thinking is subtle but important:

> **Janusian:** “How can both X and not-X be true?”
> **Bisociation:** “What happens if I take the rule of X and make it operate in Y?”

# 3. Modern homospatial thinking

**The homospatial move:** *mentally put two distinct entities into the same conceptual/spatial location, allowing a new identity to emerge.*

This is the most unusual of Rothenberg's three mechanisms. His canonical scientific example is **Joshua Lederberg**, who described mentally superimposing himself on a bacterium in thinking through bacterial conjugation. ([Psychology Today][11])

|  # | Nobel work / laureate                                     | Entity A                | Entity B                         | Homospatial fusion                                         | Prose reconstruction                                                                                                                        |
| -: | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|  1 | **Lederberg — bacterial conjugation**                     | Human observer          | Bacterium                        | Observer inhabits bacterial perspective                    | **“Put myself inside the bacterium and the mystery changes: what would I need to exchange genetic information?”**                           |
|  2 | **Einstein — equivalence principle**                      | Falling observer        | Gravitational field              | Observer + inertial frame occupy same conceptual scene     | **“Put the observer and the falling object in the same frame, and gravity begins to disappear.”**                                           |
|  3 | **Bohr — complementarity**                                | Wave picture            | Particle picture                 | Two descriptions superimposed on one quantum phenomenon    | **“Place the wave and particle images on the same object and the contradiction becomes the phenomenon.”**                                   |
|  4 | **Schrödinger — wave mechanics**                          | Particle                | Wave                             | Matter represented as a spatial wave                       | **“Put the particle into wave-space and its motion becomes a field rather than a trajectory.”**                                             |
|  5 | **de Broglie — matter waves**                             | Matter                  | Wave                             | Material object receives wave identity                     | **“Put a wavelength onto matter and matter acquires a second physical identity.”**                                                          |
|  6 | **Dirac — particle/antiparticle**                         | Particle                | Hole/absence                     | Absence treated as a physical entity                       | **“Put the missing state into the particle picture and the absence behaves like a particle.”**                                              |
|  7 | **Feynman — antiparticle interpretation**                 | Particle moving forward | Particle moving backward in time | Same entity in two temporal orientations                   | **“Put the antiparticle into the same spacetime picture as the particle and reverse its temporal direction.”**                              |
|  8 | **Hopfield — neural energy landscape**                    | Neuron network          | Physical energy landscape        | Abstract memory mapped onto spatial attractor              | **“Put a memory into a landscape and remembering becomes falling into a basin.”**                                                           |
|  9 | **Thouless — topology/material**                          | Mathematical invariant  | Physical material                | Abstract topological object inhabits matter                | **“Put topology inside the material and an abstract invariant becomes experimentally observable.”** ([Nobel Prize][6])                      |
| 10 | **Laughlin — quantum fluid**                              | Electron                | Fluid excitation                 | Individual particles represented collectively              | **“Put the electrons into a single fluid identity and fractional excitations become natural.”** ([Nobel Prize][5])                          |
| 11 | **Geim & Novoselov — graphene**                           | 3D graphite             | 2D plane                         | Bulk material compressed conceptually into an atomic sheet | **“Put ordinary graphite into a two-dimensional space and an entirely new material appears.”** ([Nobel Prize][7])                           |
| 12 | **Yamanaka — iPS cells**                                  | Mature cell             | Embryonic-like cell              | Two cellular identities occupy one cell                    | **“Put the mature and pluripotent identities into the same cell and differentiation becomes reversible.”** ([Nobel Prize][2])               |
| 13 | **Karikó & Weissman — modified mRNA**                     | Foreign molecule        | Self molecule                    | One RNA molecule occupies two immunological identities     | **“Put ‘foreign’ and ‘self’ into the same RNA molecule and change only the chemical marks.”** ([Nobel Prize][3])                            |
| 14 | **Allison & Honjo — checkpoint therapy**                  | Immune brake            | Immune weapon                    | Inhibition mechanism becomes therapeutic target            | **“Put the brake and the accelerator into the same immune system: remove the brake and the system attacks the tumor.”** ([Nobel Prize][12]) |
| 15 | **Kary Mullis — PCR**                                     | DNA replication         | Laboratory cycle                 | Biological replication enacted inside a machine            | **“Put the logic of cellular DNA replication inside a thermal cycle and amplification becomes programmable.”**                              |
| 16 | **CRISPR — Doudna & Charpentier**                         | Immune defense          | Genetic engineering              | Bacterial defense machinery becomes editing instrument     | **“Put a bacterial immune mechanism into a molecular engineering context and defense becomes editing.”**                                    |
| 17 | **AlphaFold-era protein science — Baker/Hassabis/Jumper** | Sequence                | 3D structure                     | Linear code and spatial form represented together          | **“Put the amino-acid sequence and the folded structure into the same computational object and prediction becomes possible.”**              |

The CRISPR and AlphaFold examples are **homospatial-style reconstructions**, not claims that Rothenberg documented those laureates using homospatial cognition. The Nobel work itself is well established; the cognitive classification is ours.

## The three mechanisms become almost algorithmic

|                              | What is mentally combined?       | Generative question                                     | Typical output                                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Bisociation — Koestler**   | **Two frames/domains**           | **“What if the rule from A operates in B?”**            | New analogy, mechanism, model                  |
| **Janusian — Rothenberg**    | **Two opposites**                | **“What if X and ¬X are simultaneously true?”**         | New theory resolving an apparent contradiction |
| **Homospatial — Rothenberg** | **Two entities/representations** | **“What if A and B occupy the same conceptual space?”** | New identity, representation, or object        |

And there is a very interesting progression hiding here:

**Bisociation crosses worlds.
Janusian thinking collapses opposites.
Homospatial thinking collapses entities.**

That gives you three different operators for your Eureka Engine:

> **CROSS → CONTRADICT → FUSE**

Rothenberg's work is especially valuable because these are not simply retrospective descriptions invented for famous discoveries: his research program explicitly investigated these processes among Nobel laureates and distinguished Janusian and homospatial operations as separate cognitive processes.
