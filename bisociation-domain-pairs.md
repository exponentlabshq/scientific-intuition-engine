### The structural point

The **fourth column** identifies the two matrices being brought together.

The **fifth** names the resulting idea.

The **sixth** is the most interesting: it forces the bisociation into a **single ratiocinative sentence**:

> **“I noticed that the relational rule governing X also governed Y.”**

That's stronger than saying two things are merely analogous. It identifies the **invariant structure between domains**—the thing that survives the domain change.

And that is probably the most useful operational definition of **bisociation** for the framework you're building: **not similarity between things, but the transplantation of a generative relation from one domain into another.**
