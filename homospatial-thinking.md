Rothenberg’s definition is narrower than “metaphor” or “analogy”: **Homospatial thinking means actively conceiving two or more discrete entities occupying the same space, a conception leading to the articulation of new identities**. Cognitive science later re-derived a closely related operation as **conceptual blending** (Fauconnier & Turner).

### The important distinction

Rothenberg’s strongest Homospatial cases are not merely **“A resembles B.”** They are closer to:

> **Put A and B in the same space. Do not map between them. Hold the overlay until a third identity appears that belongs to neither parent alone.**

That keeps the three mechanisms cleanly separable:

| Cognitive mechanism | Core operation | Signature sentence |
| ------------------- | -------------- | ------------------ |
| **Bisociation — Koestler** | Two previously separate matrices collide | **“What works here might work there.”** |
| **Janusian thinking — Rothenberg** | Two opposites are conceived simultaneously | **“Both of these apparently incompatible things are true.”** |
| **Homospatial thinking — Rothenberg** | Distinct entities occupy the same conceptual/spatial representation | **“Put these separate things in the same place and see what new identity emerges.”** |

Homospatial’s failure mode — the one the Eureka Engine must catch mechanically — is **bisociation wearing fusion’s name**: still two things related by “like / mirrors / akin to,” rather than **one chimera** described in its own vocabulary.
