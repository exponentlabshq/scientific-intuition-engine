## 1. The Domain

Physical gear systems are mechanical devices that transmit torque and rotational motion between different parts of a machine. They operate based on principles of leverage, friction, and mechanical advantage, allowing for the efficient transfer of energy.

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that gear systems function by maintaining a fixed ratio of input to output speeds, meaning the output speed is always a direct function of the input speed and the gear ratio.

The exact opposite is true: gear systems can function without a fixed ratio of input to output speeds, allowing for variable output speeds that do not depend on a consistent gear ratio.
