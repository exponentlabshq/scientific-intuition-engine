## 1. The Domain

In the domain of finance, transactions are fundamental actions that can either succeed or fail, influencing account balances that are often uncertain due to market fluctuations. The market itself is dynamic, evolving based on various factors, and portfolio performance is context-dependent, influenced by concurrent trading activities and regulatory frameworks.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that "market efficiency ensures that all available information is reflected in asset prices."

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: "market inefficiency ensures that not all available information is reflected in asset prices."

## 4. The Simultaneous Hold

> "Market efficiency ensures that all available information is reflected in asset prices."  
> "Market inefficiency ensures that not all available information is reflected in asset prices."  
> "Both are true simultaneously."

- **(A) Compromise**: Market efficiency applies in some contexts while market inefficiency applies in others, leading to varying asset price reflections.
- **(B) Synthesis**: The market is generally efficient, but inefficiencies exist that can be exploited by savvy investors.
- **(C) Paradox**: Market prices can be both efficient and inefficient at the same time, reflecting the dual nature of information processing in financial systems.

(C) is the paradox because it asserts that both efficiency and inefficiency coexist in the same market context, rather than merely applying to different situations or subsets. (A) fails as it suggests a context-dependent application, while (B) resolves the contradiction by favoring efficiency.

## 5. The Hypothesis (The Third Thing)

**If both market efficiency and market inefficiency hold simultaneously, then asset prices will exhibit unpredictable behavior that cannot be explained solely by the presence of complete information or its absence — which would not be predicted by either truth held alone.**
