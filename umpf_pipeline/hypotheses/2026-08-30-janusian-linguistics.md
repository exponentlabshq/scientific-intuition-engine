## 1. The Domain

Creole languages emerge in contexts where speakers of different languages need to communicate, often under conditions of social upheaval or colonization. These languages are characterized by a blend of linguistic elements from the contributing languages, resulting in a new, stable language system that serves as the primary means of communication for a community.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in the field is that creole languages develop from a pidgin stage, where simplified language forms are used as a means of communication between speakers of different native languages.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: creole languages do not develop from a pidgin stage; they arise directly from the interaction of native speakers of multiple languages without a simplified intermediary form.
