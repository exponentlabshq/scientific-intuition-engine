## 1. The Domain

In common law systems, judicial decisions from previous cases (precedents) are used as a guide for deciding current cases, a principle known as stare decisis. This creates a consistent legal framework where similar cases are treated similarly, ensuring predictability and stability in the law.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that adherence to precedent (stare decisis) is essential for maintaining the integrity and reliability of the legal system.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: adherence to precedent (stare decisis) undermines the integrity and reliability of the legal system.

## 4. The Simultaneous Hold

> "Adherence to precedent is essential for maintaining the integrity and reliability of the legal system."  
> "Adherence to precedent undermines the integrity and reliability of the legal system."  
> "Both are true simultaneously."

- **(A) Compromise**: Adherence to precedent is important in many cases, but there are instances where flexibility is necessary for justice.
- **(B) Synthesis**: A balanced approach to precedent allows for both stability and adaptability in the legal system.
- **(C) Paradox**: The legal system is both strengthened and weakened by adherence to precedent simultaneously, as it provides stability while also potentially perpetuating unjust outcomes.

(C) is the paradox because it captures the simultaneous truth of both propositions without resolving the contradiction. (A) fails because it suggests a conditional application of precedent, which does not hold for all cases. (B) resolves the tension by suggesting a middle ground, which is not truly Janusian.

## 5. The Hypothesis (The Third Thing)

**"If both adherence to precedent strengthens the legal system and undermines it simultaneously, then we should observe cases where following precedent leads to both consistent rulings and significant injustices — which would not be predicted by either truth held alone."**
