## 1. The Domain

Human cognitive bias refers to the systematic patterns of deviation from norm or rationality in judgment, leading individuals to make illogical decisions or interpret information in a skewed manner. These biases affect various aspects of human behavior, including decision-making, perception, and memory.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in the study of cognitive bias is that cognitive biases are inherent flaws in human reasoning that consistently lead to errors in judgment.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: cognitive biases are inherent strengths in human reasoning that consistently lead to accurate judgments.

> "Cognitive biases are inherent flaws in human reasoning that consistently lead to errors in judgment."

> "Both are true simultaneously."

(C) is the paradox because it asserts that cognitive biases can operate as both errors and strengths at the same time, while (A) and (B) fail to hold the contradiction simultaneously, instead suggesting a conditional relationship.

**If both cognitive biases are inherent flaws in human reasoning and cognitive biases are inherent strengths in human reasoning, then individuals will exhibit both irrational decision-making and effective decision-making in the same context, depending on the complexity of the information presented — which would not be predicted by either truth held alone.**
