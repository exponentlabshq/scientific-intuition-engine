## 1. The Domain

Human meeting participation involves individuals collaborating to share ideas, make decisions, and solve problems within various contexts, such as workplaces, educational settings, or community groups. The effectiveness of meetings can be influenced by the level of participation from attendees, which can vary significantly.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that higher levels of participation in meetings lead to more effective decision-making outcomes.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: lower levels of participation in meetings lead to more effective decision-making outcomes.

## 4. The Simultaneous Hold

> "Lower levels of participation in meetings lead to more effective decision-making outcomes."  
> "Both are true simultaneously."

- **(A) Compromise**: Effective decision-making outcomes depend on varying levels of participation, suggesting that sometimes more voices enhance decisions while other times fewer voices streamline the process.
- **(B) Synthesis**: There is an optimal level of participation that balances the benefits of diverse input with the efficiency of fewer contributors.
- **(C) Paradox**: Effective decision-making can simultaneously arise from both the collaborative richness of high participation and the focused clarity of low participation in the same meeting context.

(C) is the paradox here because it asserts that both increased and decreased participation can lead to effective decision-making outcomes at the same time within the same meeting. (A) and (B) fail to be genuinely Janusian because they resolve the contradiction by suggesting a compromise or an average rather than maintaining the tension of both extremes being true at once.
