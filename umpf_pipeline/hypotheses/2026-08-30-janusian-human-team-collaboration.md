## 1. The Domain

Human team collaboration involves individuals working together towards shared objectives, utilizing diverse skills and perspectives to enhance creativity and problem-solving. It is often characterized by interactions that can either facilitate or hinder the effectiveness of the team's performance.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that effective collaboration among team members leads to better performance and outcomes.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: ineffective collaboration among team members leads to better performance and outcomes.

## 4. The Simultaneous Hold

> "Effective collaboration among team members leads to better performance and outcomes."  
> "Ineffective collaboration among team members leads to better performance and outcomes."  
> "Both are true simultaneously."

- **(A) Compromise**: Effective collaboration is beneficial, while ineffective collaboration is detrimental, leading to a clear distinction in outcomes based on the type of collaboration.
- **(B) Synthesis**: Collaboration can be both effective and ineffective, but the overall performance is determined by a blend of both types, averaging the outcomes.
- **(C) Paradox**: Effective collaboration can yield high performance through structured teamwork, while ineffective collaboration can also yield high performance by fostering innovation through chaos and conflict, as the disarray of poor collaboration can lead to unexpected breakthroughs.

(C) is the paradox because it holds both propositions true at once for the same instance. (A) fails as it suggests a clear distinction in outcomes, while (B) averages the results, neither of which preserves the inherent contradiction.

## 5. The Hypothesis (The Third Thing)

1. **Simultaneous-hold sentence (required)**: "Both effective and ineffective collaboration among team members are true simultaneously for the same team; the theory must contain both."
2. **Falsifiable prediction**: "If both effective collaboration and ineffective collaboration hold simultaneously for the same instance, then teams that experience both structured teamwork and chaotic interactions will produce innovative solutions that outperform teams with exclusively effective collaboration — which neither truth alone predicts."
