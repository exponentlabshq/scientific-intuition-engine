## 1. The Domain

Behavioral psychology studies how habits are formed through loops that consist of cues, routines, and rewards. This framework helps explain how certain behaviors become automatic responses in specific contexts, leading to habitual actions over time.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in this field is that habits are formed through a consistent loop of cue, routine, and reward, which reinforces the behavior over time.

The exact opposite is true: habits are formed without any consistent loop of cue, routine, and reward, and behaviors can emerge randomly without reinforcement.

(C) is the paradox because it asserts that an individual can experience both structured habit formation and random behavior emergence simultaneously, which (A) fails to do by suggesting a context-dependent approach, and (B) resolves the contradiction by averaging the two views rather than preserving the tension.
