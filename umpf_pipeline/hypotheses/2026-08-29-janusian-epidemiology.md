## 1. The Domain

In epidemiology, herd immunity refers to the resistance to the spread of a contagious disease within a population when a sufficient proportion of individuals are immune, either through vaccination or previous infections. The herd immunity threshold (HIT) is the critical point at which enough people are immune to prevent further transmission of the disease.

## 2. The Proposition

The load-bearing assumption in epidemiology is that there exists a specific herd immunity threshold that, once reached, effectively halts the spread of infectious diseases in a population.

## 3. The Inversion

The exact opposite is true: there is no specific herd immunity threshold that can universally halt the spread of infectious diseases in a population.

(C) is the paradox because it captures the truth that while specific thresholds exist for certain diseases, the variability and complexity of human populations and disease interactions mean that there is no one-size-fits-all threshold. (A) fails because it suggests a simple variation rather than a simultaneous truth, and (B) collapses the contradiction into a generalized statement, thus losing the tension.
