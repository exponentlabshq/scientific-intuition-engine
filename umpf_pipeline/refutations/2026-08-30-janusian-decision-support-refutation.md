- **Coherence — REFUTED.** The hypothesis claims a same-instance paradox, but the core claim is context-dependent: it describes different outcomes based on the accuracy of advice, not a true paradox. The simultaneous-hold sentence equivocates on 'decision-making outcomes' by treating accurate and inaccurate advice as the same mechanism, which they are not. This is a disguised context-dependent compromise, not a genuine Janusian paradox.
- **Testability — REFUTED.** The hypothesis lacks a clear operationalized falsifiable prediction. The claim that a decision can result in both a successful and a failed outcome simultaneously is not concretely defined with a specific metric, comparison condition, or rejection threshold. Without these, the prediction remains vague and cannot be empirically tested in a way that would allow for a definitive 'no' result.
- **Triviality — REFUTED.** The hypothesis reduces to a trivial observation that any tool or system can have both positive and negative effects simultaneously, depending on its use and context. This is a common characteristic of complex systems, where outcomes are influenced by multiple interacting factors. The claim does not present a novel insight specific to decision support systems, but rather a general truth applicable to many systems, thus falling into the umbrella trap.
