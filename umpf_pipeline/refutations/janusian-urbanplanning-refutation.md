**Original**: `hypotheses/2026-08-29-janusian-urban-planning.md`
**Method**: 3 independent agents, one per lens, blind to each other

## Tally: 0 of 3 survive → **REFUTED**

The third compromise-wearing-paradox case in this batch — the same-instance test's failure mode recurring
across three independently generated Janusian hypotheses is itself a notable pattern (see the reconciled
batch summary).

- **Coherence — REFUTED.** §4C's "order in SOME contexts... stifling... in OTHERS" partitions the claim
  across different urban contexts rather than asserting one zoning regime is simultaneously order-creating
  and chaos-creating in the same instance. §7 confirms this directly: "different types of urban areas...
  actually describing separate phenomena."
- **Testability — REFUTED.** Virtually every city on Earth — zoned or unzoned, strict or lax — already
  shows both organized and informal/disorganized land use. No threshold, ratio, or control condition is
  specified that would let any city's data fail to "confirm" the claim; the confirming instance is the base
  rate of urban development everywhere.
- **Triviality — REFUTED.** "Any binding regulation trades predictability for foreclosed flexibility" is
  the generic regulation-tradeoff observation, applicable equally to tax law, safety codes, or immigration
  policy — nothing here is specific to zoning's actual instruments (density limits, use-segregation,
  variance processes).
