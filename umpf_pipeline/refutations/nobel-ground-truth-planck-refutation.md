- **Coherence — SURVIVES.** The core claim rests on the shared term 'energy exchange,' which refers to the same formal concept in both domains: the quantization of energy in Planck's theory and the thermodynamic behavior of blackbody radiation. In both contexts, energy exchange is treated as a discrete process, allowing for a coherent mapping between the two domains without equivocation. The claim successfully demonstrates how the introduction of quantization resolves the ultraviolet catastrophe, maintaining the integrity of both domains.
- **Testability — SURVIVES.** The claim explicitly names a specific historical experiment and result, namely Planck's resolution of the ultraviolet catastrophe through the introduction of energy quanta (E = hf). This is a checkable piece of evidence that operationalizes the hypothesis, satisfying the testability lens without requiring additional metrics or thresholds.
- **Triviality — REFUTED.** The core claim states that the observed blackbody radiation spectrum cannot be derived from classical physics' assumption of continuous energy exchange, and that quantized energy exchange reproduces the observed spectrum. When stripped of domain-specific vocabulary, this reduces to a statement about the behavior of energy exchange in complex systems. The phrase being tested for genericness is: 'The observed spectrum cannot be derived from the assumption that energy exchange is continuous.' This is a general statement that could apply to many complex systems, thus failing the triviality lens.
