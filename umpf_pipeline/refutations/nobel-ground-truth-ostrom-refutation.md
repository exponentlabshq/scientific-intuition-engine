In institutional economics, governance pertains to the rules and structures that facilitate collective action, while in ecology, it refers to the mechanisms that sustain resource use without depletion. Both domains analyze how communities organize themselves to manage shared resources effectively, demonstrating a coherent structural mapping without equivocation.

This constitutes a specific, checkable piece of evidence that operationalizes the hypothesis, satisfying the testability lens. The claim is not vague and clearly outlines the mechanisms through which local communities can manage resources sustainably.

When stripped of domain-specific vocabulary, the claim reduces to a general statement about complex systems managing shared resources, which is true of many systems and lacks the specificity required to be a unique or novel insight. The phrase being tested for genericness is: 'local communities repeatedly self-organize durable governance institutions that sustainably manage common-pool resources without either privatization or centralized regulation.' This is indeed a broader statement applicable to various complex systems, thus failing the triviality test.
