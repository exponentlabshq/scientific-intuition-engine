# Adversarial Refutation: Bisociation — Epidemiology × Creative Inspiration Variability

**Original**: `hypotheses/2026-08-29-epidemiology-x-creative-inspiration-variability.md`
**Method**: 3 independent agents, one per lens, blind to each other

## Tally: 0 of 3 survive → **REFUTED**

All three lenses converged: "threshold" is a formally derived quantity in M1 (herd immunity threshold =
1 − 1/R0) and an undefined placeholder in M2.

- **Coherence — REFUTED.** M2's "threshold" has no formula, no unit, no derivation, and §4 doesn't even
  commit to a causal direction ("or vice versa"). The deeper equivocation is "interaction through infection
  and immunity" vs. "interaction through collaboration and feedback" — infection dynamics are
  contagion/depletion (a susceptible pool shrinks as immunity spreads); creative feedback has no analog of
  becoming "immune" to further inspiration. The mapping works only if "spreads through a population" is
  read as a generic metaphor, not the specific SIR-style mechanism that gives herd immunity its meaning.
- **Testability — REFUTED.** No percentage, headcount, output rate, or measurement protocol is ever given
  for "a certain threshold of collaborative creative input." Combined with the "or vice versa" hedge, any
  pattern in either direction confirms the claim — there is no experiment whose negative result the
  hypothesis is committed to accepting.
- **Triviality — REFUTED.** Stripped of vocabulary: "past some critical mass, a group-level outcome
  changes" — true of riots, quorum sensing, viral marketing, and network effects generally. No
  epidemiology-specific mechanism (R0, contact-rate thresholds, immunity decay) actually ports onto
  creativity; only the English word "threshold" does.

## No steelman offered

No lens found a narrower rescuable claim. A real version would need an actual operational definition of a
"creative collaboration threshold" grounded in existing creativity research (e.g., team-size effects on
idea generation, which is a real, measured literature) rather than an analogy to epidemiological
percentages.
