- **Coherence — REFUTED.** §3's "Phonetic Turbulence" doesn't introduce a new mechanism; it relabels "sound
  change has both regular and irregular patterns" with fluid-dynamics vocabulary. No shared causal variable
  (no Reynolds-number analog, no viscosity/velocity-threshold equivalent) does actual explanatory work.
- **Testability — REFUTED.** No Reynolds-number analog, no critical threshold distinguishing "laminar" from
  "turbulent" sound change, no fluid-dynamics equation mapped onto phonetic variables. "Measurable through
  phonetic analysis" gestures at standard sociolinguistic methodology already in use — not a new predictive
  apparatus. Regular and irregular sound change are both already established facts in historical
  linguistics, so no dataset could fail to "confirm" this.
- **Triviality — REFUTED.** Strip "turbulence" and the claim reduces to: sound change is sometimes gradual,
  sometimes sudden, and correlates with social factors — true of stock markets, epidemics, evolution, or
  technology adoption. Substitute "avalanche" or "punctuated equilibrium" for "turbulence" and the
  hypothesis is unchanged.
