## What was found
The concept of entropy in thermodynamics is central to understanding the direction and irreversibility of natural processes. The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease over time, leading to the concept of the 'arrow of time' that dictates the direction of processes. However, this law applies to the system as a whole and does not preclude local decreases in entropy within subsystems, provided the total entropy of the entire system increases. This is evident in nonequilibrium thermodynamics, where local decreases in entropy can occur in parts of a system, such as in the formation of dissipative structures, as long as the overall entropy production is positive. ([pubs.acs.org](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/jp962650o?utm_source=openai))
