## What was found
The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) posits that asset prices fully reflect all available information, suggesting that markets are efficient. However, numerous market anomalies, such as the momentum effect, January effect, and post-earnings-announcement drift, challenge this notion by demonstrating predictable patterns in asset prices that contradict EMH. Behavioral finance introduces psychological factors, like overconfidence and loss aversion, to explain these anomalies, indicating that both market efficiency and inefficiency can coexist, leading to unpredictable asset price behavior.
