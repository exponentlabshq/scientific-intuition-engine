## What was found
The spacing effect, first identified by Ebbinghaus, demonstrates that distributing learning sessions over time enhances long-term retention compared to massed practice. ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3399982/?utm_source=openai)) This principle is widely accepted in educational psychology. Additionally, the forgetting curve illustrates the decline of memory retention over time, emphasizing the importance of spaced repetition to counteract forgetting. ([cognitivepsychology.com](https://www.cognitivepsychology.com/Forgetting_Curve?utm_source=openai))

## Reasoning
The hypothesis that both spaced repetition and its absence can simultaneously lead to effective long-term retention contradicts established research. The spacing effect and forgetting curve collectively indicate that spaced repetition is essential for optimal memory retention, making the simultaneous effectiveness of both practices implausible.
