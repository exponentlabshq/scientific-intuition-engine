## Reasoning
Gravitational lensing involves the bending of light due to massive objects, as predicted by Einstein's theory of general relativity. This phenomenon is mathematically described by the deflection angle formula: α = 4GM/(c²b), where G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the lensing object, c is the speed of light, and b is the impact parameter. In optics, diffraction patterns arise when light encounters obstacles or slits, leading to constructive and destructive interference. The angular position of the m-th diffraction minimum is given by: sin(θ) = mλ/d, where λ is the wavelength of light, d is the distance between slits, and m is the diffraction order. While both phenomena involve the bending of light, the underlying causes and mathematical treatments differ significantly.
