The Eureka Engine

A machine that manufactures scientific hunches on purpose, thousands at a time, and checks each one honestly before anyone spends months finding out by hand.

Exponent Labs LLC · 2026-09-01 · scientific-intuition-engine/umpf_pipeline

Abstract

Most scientific breakthroughs come from colliding two unrelated fields, not staring harder at one — Darwin reading an economics pamphlet produced natural selection; Einstein imagining a falling man led toward general relativity. The Eureka Engine automates that collision on purpose, using three documented creativity mechanisms, then checks each result against real evidence before anyone spends months finding out by hand.

Across all 755 hypotheses it has generated, the engine's own confidence tracks reality: its most-confident ideas independently match real, existing research 71% of the time; even the ideas it rejects still match real research 36% of the time — checked against its entire output, not a sample. Its adversarial “kill it” check was itself tested against 13 real Nobel-linked discoveries. The first pass rejected all of them — evidence the checker was broken, not that real science looks like nonsense. Fixed and validated, it now correctly recognizes 61.5% of real discoveries while still rejecting 291 of 291 real machine-generated candidates. Twenty real engineering failures were found and fixed along the way, reported here rather than smoothed over. Total spend to date: $6.75, entirely on OpenAI tokens.

1What This Is, in One Story

In 1838, Charles Darwin picked up an economics pamphlet with nothing to do with finches or beetles — Thomas Malthus's essay on how populations outgrow food supply, guaranteeing that most of every generation dies before it can reproduce. Darwin had spent two years puzzling over why related species on nearby islands differ in small, specific ways. Reading Malthus, it clicked: if every population is born into a competition it mostly loses, individuals with any small edge survive that competition more often, generation after generation. No one has to design anything. You get evolution by natural selection.

Darwin named the collision himself: reading Malthus is where the idea “at once occurred to me.” The most consequential idea in the history of biology came not from staring harder at finches, but from forcing two unrelated fields into the same sentence. Kepler tried to explain planetary orbits through musical harmony; Shannon founded information theory by noticing that telephone-relay logic and Boolean algebra were structurally the same thing. The pattern recurs often enough to ask a genuinely strange question: what if the collision itself — not talent, not luck — is the actual mechanism, and it can be done on purpose?

A hypothesis, in this report, is a specific, falsifiable guess about how two things are secretly connected — precise enough that it could turn out to be wrong. The Eureka Engine automates both halves of what Darwin did once, by accident: force the collision on purpose, across a large domain pool, thousands of times — then honestly check each result the way a skeptical colleague would, before anyone spends a year finding out by hand.

2Three Ways to Force a Collision

Two researchers, working decades apart, independently described three distinct ways minds produce insights like Darwin's. The Eureka Engine implements all three as separate, mechanical generation modes.

MechanismNamed byWhat it forcesReal example
BisociationArthur Koestler, The Act of Creation (1964)Two unrelated domains, collided into one precise, falsifiable mappingDarwin + Malthus → natural selection
JanusianAlbert Rothenberg, from direct study of Nobel laureatesOne domain's core assumption held true and false, simultaneouslyEinstein's falling man — weightless and accelerating at once — toward general relativity
HomospatialAlbert RothenbergTwo domains superimposed into one new entity, not a metaphorRothenberg's own experiment: subjects shown two photographs superimposed produced more original metaphors than subjects shown the same photos side by side

A functor — borrowed loosely from category theory — just means a stated, checkable rule for translating a concept from one domain into its counterpart in the other. “Overcrowding in market X should produce the same boom-bust cycle observed in population Y” is falsifiable; “markets are like ecosystems” is not. The pipeline enforces that precision mechanically — homospatial output, for instance, is scanned and rejected if it's secretly just a metaphor wearing a coined name.

None of this required a machine, in principle — Darwin and Einstein each did it with an unaided mind, once, after years of immersion in one field. What a machine changes is scale: thousands of deliberate collisions against a domain pool of 109 real fields, instead of one lucky accident in a career.

None of these numbers are about the Eureka Engine — they describe the ordinary, already-existing cost of human research. Verification (Section 5) and refutation (Section 6) exist to compress a year of that discovery into minutes and a handful of search queries — not a substitute for real experimental work on a hypothesis that survives, but a way to catch, cheaply, the ones that would have failed anyway.

4The Pipeline

Generation is Phase 1 of four:

  • Exploration — the three mechanisms above, drawing domain pairs from the pool.
  • Web Verification — a four-way classifier checks each hypothesis against real search results (Section 5).
  • Researcher Outreach — for hypotheses that clear verification, draft (never auto-send) a short email to a real, named researcher in the adjacent field.
  • Data Update — reconcile whatever outreach reveals back into the hypothesis's status.
No email is ever sent automatically Three drafts were reviewed and sent by hand on 2026-08-29, to real researchers (Igor Aronson, Erwin Frey, John B. Phillips) — the 21-day reply clock is still running. Two more sit send-ready; two still need a human's own concreteness judgment.

Generation, verification, and refutation now run as one unattended command — python3 run_cycle.py --total N — with no live session required (Section 7).

5Verification

Phase 2 is a fact-checker: has someone already said this, does real unclaimed territory sit nearby, or does the premise not even hold up? Every result sorts into one of four outcomes, not pass/fail — because “did the search find something” collapses two opposite signals. Finding the exact idea already published means zero marginal novelty. Finding real, active research nearby, without the exact connection drawn, is the actual target: fertile, unclaimed ground.

The umbrella-trap rule A bridging claim only counts as real evidence if it's specific enough that it wouldn't return the same hit for most other domain pairs. “Both are complex adaptive systems” is true of nearly any two nonlinear domains — a real case exactly like this was caught and correctly routed to NO_SIGNAL rather than a false-positive promotion.

6Adversarial Refutation — Can It Be Trusted?

If Phase 2 is a fact-checker, this phase is a hostile peer-review panel: three independent AI reviewers, each blind to the other two, each trying to find the one flaw that kills the claim.

  • Coherence — does the mapping equivocate on a term as it crosses domains?
  • Testability — is the prediction actually operationalized?
  • Triviality — stripped of jargon, does the claim reduce to something true of almost any two complex systems?

Each lens defaults to REFUTED under uncertainty; promotion requires 2 of 3 to survive. For nearly this project's entire history, refutation had never once said SURVIVES — 0 of 245 real attempts. That perfect record was genuinely ambiguous: a mechanism that correctly kills everything bad looks identical to one that simply can't discriminate at all.

The test: 13 real Nobel-linked discoveries, run through the same gauntlet

Real, historically-confirmed claims — Kahneman & Tversky, Nash, Hopfield, Planck, and nine more — were run through the identical gauntlet every generated hypothesis gets. First pass: 0 of 5 survived. Real bugs, not bad luck: Triviality called Hopfield's claim — the literal subject of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics — “a common characteristic of complex systems.” A first fix scored 3 of 5, but only on the cases used to find the bugs; a fresh, non-overlapping batch of 8 more came back 2 of 8 — the fix had memorized its examples, not the rule. Checked the other direction at 20× the scale: every one of the 271 hypotheses this pipeline had ever generated and refuted was re-run under the corrected lenses. Zero flipped. One more real finding: the 0/1/2/3-of-3 vote itself, previously collapsed to a binary, turned out to matter. Five of the historically-confirmed discoveries still marked REFUTED are genuine 1-of-3 near-misses, not unanimous rejections — a distinction the leaderboard's tiers now reflect (Section 10).

7Full Autonomy

Generation, verification, and refutation run as one command: python3 run_cycle.py --total N, no live session required. publish_site.py then rebuilds the public leaderboard and dashboard automatically; this report stays a human-reviewed pass.

Fail-closed, not fail-quiet Any stage that errors marks the cycle DEGRADED and skips publish — verified twice for real, including once when the Janusian domain pool genuinely ran dry mid-cycle. The pool has since grown from 59 to 109 domains.

9The Audit Agent

audit_agent.py runs lightweight every cycle (one grounded observation, ~1,300 tokens) and occasionally proposes one specific, additive improvement. It cannot edit or delete a file, only propose new ones — adoption is always a deliberate human act. Four real proposals exist: two had real bugs caught before adoption; one remains unreviewed; one was hand-authored to fix a gap the auditor's own grounding couldn't see (domain-pool exhaustion, Section 7), and was adopted directly.

10The Leaderboard — Tiers and Scoring

The scoring formula was rearchitected after two real bugs surfaced. Self-reported novelty (near-zero predictive signal, confirmed separately) was still being scored by default — fixed; it now shows as context only, not points. Flat points let a REFUTED entry outrank a real SURVIVES entry, confirmed directly against the live ledger — fixed with six confidence tiers (Peer-Endorsed → Survived Refutation → Verified/Unrefuted → Pending → Contested → Refuted/Rejected), ranked tier-first, points only breaking ties within a tier. Ground-truth Nobel entries were also found to be earning “discovery credit” they hadn't earned — fixed; their refutation-survival points are untouched.

Does the engine's own best output independently match real research?

All 8 real SURVIVES entries were checked against real, existing literature — not whether the engine's own check approves, but whether an actual researcher, paper, or project addresses the same mechanism. 6 of 8 (75%) returned a real, citable match: a 1997 paper on the exact compiler tradeoff it proposed; three real papers on animal navigation combining instinct and learning; real magnetic-fluctuation research matching its Kalman-filtering claim. This is not evidence the engine discovered anything — every match predates the hypothesis. It is evidence the engine's ideas land in real territory far above chance.

12Pointing the Engine at Real Discoveries, Blind

A harder test: pointed at the exact domain pairs behind seven more real Nobel-linked discoveries (Marconi, Fleming, Prigogine, two Black-Scholes framings, Nash/Malthus, Watson-Crick/information theory), blind to the historical answer, can generation land anywhere near it on its own?

2 of 7 landed clean. Prigogine's blind attempt (“energy exchanges... local interactions... global emergent behavior”) recognizably matches the real “energy flowing through a system can create structure.” The Watson-Crick attempt is genuinely close to “a molecule that specifies how another is built is an information-bearing code.” Four were refuted; two genuinely missed the mechanism — Marconi's attempt reduced to generic “signal management,” never reaching that wireless removes the need for a wire at all.

A real, disclosed limitation The generating model's training data plausibly already contains real facts about these cases — a hit is not clean proof of independent reasoning, it could partly reflect memorized recall.

13The v2 Pre-Filter

A pre-filter checks whether a proposed cross-domain connection actually composes — extends to a second, independent hop, not just holds for one — before a real generation call gets spent.

The gold-standard test

Against 80 real historical cross-domain discoveries, the zero-signal rate split cleanly by category:

Pair categorynZero-signal rate
Engineering / invention history140%
Economics / social science1619%
Biology / evolution-adjacent2438%
Pure physics / astronomy / chemistry1553%
Exact-shared-equation pairs956%

It misses precisely on connections a physicist would find most rigorous (Shannon entropy and thermodynamic entropy are the same equation), never on narrative-shaped ones. A cheap classifier now routes narrative/mixed pairs through the pre-filter and skips it for formalism-shaped pairs. Live correlation with real outcomes: narrative-shaped pairs land a good result 87.5% of the time vs. 70.4% for mixed-uncertain — the same split the benchmark predicted, confirmed on production data. The pre-filter remains a net cost ($0.0084 vs. $0.0024) since it reorders the queue rather than deleting from it.

Closing the formalism-shaped blind spot

Formalism-shaped pairs skip the pre-filter by design, so a separate check — a category-theoretic checklist elevating a two-term analogy to a three-term one — was built and tested. Run against all 11 live formalism-shaped entries: 2 of 11 (18%) STRONG, far above the near-zero background rate elsewhere. Not yet wired into automatic generation.

Mechanically-targeted, “confirmed formalism-shaped” pairs produced 0 of 20 STRONG hypotheses on fresh generation. A small, hand-picked set grounded in specific, named mathematical correspondences (the electro-mechanical analogy, Kalman filtering applied to magnetic fields) produced 2 of 13 (15%) — both from the same damped-harmonic-oscillator family, which tested exhaustively across all 15 possible pairs settled at 2 of 15 (13%). The lesson: classification tells you what's plausible; real domain knowledge tells you what's real.

Repairing, not just checking

A real diagnosis of one rejected hypothesis — a vibration/circuit pairing that mapped displacement to voltage, a vocabulary swap that says nothing — named a general pattern. Cross-domain claims come in three levels: lexical analogy (unfalsifiable), relational analogy, and structural homomorphism (an explicit mapping where one domain's equation actually transforms into the other's, checkable both mathematically and against literature). A new tool attempts the strongest level directly on any rejected, formalism-shaped hypothesis, honestly declining when no real mapping exists rather than forcing one.

The Failure 19→20 chain is worth a sentence on its own: fixing a real verifier bug that had let 101 hypotheses get promoted on evidence the model itself called generic almost cost this report its own Nobel-benchmark discoveries, when the fix's ledger write briefly dropped 161 real refutation results from view — including 9 of the leaderboard's 15 survivors. Disclosed rather than patched on the strength of one example (Section 16).

By design, not a gap

  • The self-audit agent observes and proposes; it does not adopt without a human act.
  • Verification and refutation remain a check on plausibility, not a substitute for a real experiment.

Deliberately not attempted

  • Nothing schedules the engine yet — every real cycle to date was started by a human, a standing decision, not a code gap.

17Conclusion

The verification layer answers two questions at once: is one hypothesis novel, and does the underlying mechanism reliably find real cross-domain territory at all? 62 of 755 hypotheses have collided with genuine prior art; 509 sit in fertile, unclaimed territory; and a direct check of the engine's strongest candidates found 6 of 8 landing in the same territory as real, existing scholarship — not proof the engine discovers anything, but real evidence its sense of where fertile territory is happens to be right more often than chance.

The harder question — can the adversarial check that's supposed to separate real hypotheses from noise be trusted at all — is no longer open the way it was. Tested against 13 real, historically-confirmed discoveries, fixed twice, and validated the other direction against every hypothesis the pipeline has ever generated, it now has a real, disclosed shape: strict on the unvindicated, capable on the real thing. The project's working thesis — that generating successful bisociations is the beginning of a university — has been tested rather than assumed. A machine that can run Darwin's collision on purpose, thousands of times, and prove its own checking mechanism can tell a real discovery from a hollow one, does not replace the scientist. It replaces the coin flip.
