For Investors & Grant Officers

Three departments each produce dozens of new research leads a week. Nobody outside FU has time to read all of them. This office exists to turn that pile into one ranked, defensible answer to the only question that matters: which of these is worth real money?

How a Recommendation Actually Gets Made

  1. Verification checks a claim against real published literature — not vibes, real search queries and real citations.
  2. Adversarial refutation puts survivors in front of three independent reviewers, blind to each other, each trying to kill the claim on coherence, testability, or triviality.
  3. Scoring converts the result into one ranked tier — the same tiers you'd use to decide a grant, from “Survived Refutation” down to “Refuted / Rejected.”

Real thing: the Charter, Articles V–VI & VIII.

## 3. The Candidate Functor

The proposed mapping *f: M(Social Systems) → M(Human Team Collaboration)* is as follows:

  • - Atomic: Uncertain interaction outcomes in social systems map to uncertain collaboration outcomes in teams.
  • - Domain: Evolving social networks correspond to evolving team compositions and roles.
  • - Control: External factors impacting social interactions map to external influences on team collaboration.
  • - Orchestration: Governance structures in social systems correspond to formal team structures and collaboration strategies.

For this functor to hold, both domains must demonstrate that their evolving structures (social networks and team compositions) significantly influence the outcomes of interactions and collaborations.

## 4. The Hypothesis
